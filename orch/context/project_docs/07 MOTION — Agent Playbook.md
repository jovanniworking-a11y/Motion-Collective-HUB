## 1. Editing (sources 3, 4)

- **Story first.** "Your main purpose is to be a storyteller." Every piece needs a beginning, middle and end. For MOTION: arrival → peak → the date. Cut any shot that doesn't move it forward.
- **Shot sequence.** "Get a few clips doing one action… cut all of our clips so they're the same length… stitch all of the clips so that they flow together in motion."
  - MOTION example: a hand on the door, then a hand on the jog wheel, then a hand on the pour. Each is 0.6 s and the hand moves the same direction across the cut.
- **Music before footage.** Pick the track first and shoot to its BPM.
  - Use the song's dips for transitions. The blackout sits in the dip before the drop.
- **Sound beats picture for retention.** Balance the music, the room sound and the hits. A bad mix kills a good cut.
- **Pacing.**
  - Fast cuts for peaks: the build and the drop.
  - Slow holds for feeling: the eyes and the balcony.
- **Less is more.** One grade, hard cuts, no app transitions, no stacked effects.
- **Grade.**
  - Expose properly first ("adding light" means more information).
  - Then apply one consistent grade across every clip. Colour sets the mood.
- **Composition.** Hierarchy, size, position and contrast tell the eye where to go. Have one subject per frame.
- **Loop.** Practise, review, iterate. Every agent ends with a self-critique against this list.

## 2. Compositing: mixing AI and real footage (source 2)

- Compositing means "assembling filmed and rendered elements from multiple sources to create a final lifelike illusion". MOTION does this whenever a Higgsfield clip sits next to phone footage.
- **Match the plates.** AI and real clips need the same:
  - grain
  - grade
  - frame rate (30 fps)
  - camera move direction
  - light colour (ice / red)

  Otherwise the AI clip reads as fake.
- **Plan the hard shots.**
  - For eyes over footage or a projector look: shoot a clean plate (the wall with nobody in front) plus the same wall with the crowd.
  - Key or rotoscope the people back over the effect.
- **Paint out clutter.** Remove exit signs, cables, the TV standby light, and logos on clothes or cans.
- **Tracking.** If text has to stick to a wall or the deck, track it (CapCut's "track" or Resolve's 2D tracker). Never float it loosely.
- **Compositing is the last step.** Lock the edit before anyone spends time on effects.

## 3. Graphics (source 1). Apply selectively.

The GFX guide is written for busy, maximal banners. MOTION's taste is the opposite: hairline lettering, no deep-fried photos, and red only as an accent. So take these lessons:

- **Contrast through complementary colour.** Ice blue against red is our complementary pair. Use it for separation, not decoration.
- **Texture from patterns.** Halftone and grain add depth. Our eyes are already halftone, so a light grain layer is on-brand.
- **Composition.**
  - Rule of thirds for single subjects.
  - **Triangle composition** for groups: one primary focus and two secondary ones. Use it for the crew lineup and for the eyes plus title plus date.
- **Separation.** Use a thin shadow or outline layer so type never sinks into the photo.
- **Blend modes.**
  - **Add** (linear dodge) for the red and ice glows.
  - **Soft Light** for mood washes over footage.
- **Colour transitions.** A faint third colour between two main colours smooths the gradient. For us, a little oxblood between red and black.

Leave out: multi-coloured letters, liquified or bent text, chains and sparkles, busy multi-colour backgrounds, and "fill every negative space". All of these conflict with client taste.

## 4. Marketing (sources 5, 6, 7)

### Positioning
- **Position against.** Liquid Death and Death Wish Coffee win by maxing out the counterculture.
- MOTION positions against Sixth Street bars and ticketed clubs: invite only, free, curated sound, a look, 70 people.
- Elevator line: **"MOTION is a collective, not a club. 70 passes, all black, the sound you'd fly out for."**

### Funnel
1. **Awareness:** teasers. Eyes with no caption, then "10.30".
2. **Interest:** the flyer and the artist spotlights.
3. **Soft call to action:** "DM 'NIGHT' to get on the list". This comes before passes open, because "soft calls to action help enable strong calls to action".
4. **Hard call to action:** a numbered pass, confirmed in the DM.
5. **Retention:** pass holders from 001 get first access to 002. Keep handles in the pass sheet and build a Close Friends list.

### Seeding and collaborations
- Send passes, and later Cap 01, to tastemakers before the public post: local DJs, photographers, scene accounts.
- Post as Instagram Collabs with the artists.
- Track all of it in one sheet. That sheet works as a mini-CRM.

### Everything is content
The pass, the cap, the dispenser labels, the eye wall and the mirror ball are each a designed photo moment. Events must be "social media friendly".

### Psychological triggers mapped to MOTION

| Trigger | What to do |
|---|---|
| Halo effect | The first post sets the bar. It has to be the best image we have (the eyes). |
| Serial position | People remember what comes first and last. Open every reel on the eyes and end on 10.30.2026 / MOTION COLLECTIVE. |
| Recency | The last post before the 30th is the strongest: the countdown plus "passes gone". |
| Mere exposure | Post often and always in the same look. Familiarity becomes trust. |
| Loss aversion | 70 numbered passes, "9 left", then "passes gone". |
| Choice overload | One call to action per post. Never "DM, link in bio and comment". |
| Framing | Say "70 passes", not "free party". "Drinks included", not "BYOB". |
| IKEA effect | Let people help make it. Run a story poll to pick the Cap 01 colourway, and let pass holders pick their number if it's free. |
| Bandwagon | Show the claimed passes, the artists' reposts and guests posting their pass. |
| Zero risk | Free, drinks included, the address sent to you, a clear end time. |
| Confirmation bias | Speak to who they already think they are: people who watch Boiler Room and dress in black. |
| Anchoring | If Cap 01 is sold, show the higher "full run" price first. |

### Serendipity (source 7)
Serendipity needs three things: **enjoyable + surprising + inexplicable**. A gift with no explanation feels bigger than an explained one.
- Post the eyes with **no caption** and don't explain.
- **Unexplained moments at the party:**
  - At midnight (30 Oct crossing into 31 Oct), the projector turns on the dilating eyes with no announcement.
  - Guests find a small card with their pass number in their cup sleeve.
  - The first 10 guests get a stamp of the eye on the wrist.
- **Leave space for people to make the meaning themselves.** Don't explain "afterimage". The brain fills the gap and remembers it.

## 5. Checklist every MOTION agent runs before handing back work (also check sections 6–7)

1. **Story:** does it have a beginning, middle and end, and does every shot earn its place?
2. **First and last frames:** eyes first, date last?
3. **Sync:** is every cut on a beat, with transitions in the song's dips?
4. **One grade, one call to action, one subject per frame?**
5. **AI clips:** do they match the real clips (grain, grade, fps, light colour)? Is clutter painted out?
6. **Client taste:** hairline type, red ≤ 10% of the frame, no deep-fry, no unconfirmed names (Glamz), no address, no YSL marks.
7. **Hype:**
   - Which trigger does this post use?
   - Is there something unexplained in it?
   - Does it fit the funnel stage of the rollout week?
8. **Self-critique:** score it 1–10 against this list, fix the lowest item, and write what you learned to `flyer_af/LESSONS.md`.

## 6. Colour psychology and colour theory (evidence-checked, 5 Oct 2026)

**Honesty rule.** Colour-psychology research is "at a nascent stage" (Elliot 2015), with small samples, few replications, and effects that change with culture and context.
- Justify colour choices by contrast, legibility, brand consistency and genre convention.
- Never claim a colour *makes* people feel or do something.

1. **Red is for attention, not seduction.** Red does win visual search. The "red makes you attractive" effect largely failed preregistered replications.
   - Use #FF3B30 for the one thing the eye must hit first: the date, the sigil or the call to action.
2. **Saturation and brightness move emotion more than hue does** (Valdez & Mehrabian 1994; Wilms & Oberfeld 2018).
   - Saturated red on near-black = high arousal. Save it for the hit moment.
   - Oxblood and pale ice carry mood quietly.
3. **Brand associations are cultural signals, not mechanisms.** Black reads premium and nocturnal, ice blue reads cold and clean ("gallery"), red reads exciting. That holds for Western club and luxury culture. It is not universal.
4. **60-30-10 (a heuristic).**
   - ~60% night #060B13
   - ~30% ice #BFDDF0 / #58C4F0 plus oxblood
   - ≤10% red
   - Apply the same split to the room: dark base, cool wash, red pinpoints.
5. **Never put red text on blue, or blue text on red.** The two colours seem to sit at different depths (chromostereopsis), so the edges vibrate. Red against cyan is only 1.79:1. Use the pair as separate fields or glows. The vibration is allowed as a deliberate glitch texture, never on information.
6. **Contrast for any info text (WCAG: 4.5:1 normal, 3:1 large).** Ratios on #060B13:

   | Colour | Ratio | Use for |
   |---|---|---|
   | White | 19.7 | anything |
   | Ice #BFDDF0 | 13.9 | text |
   | Cyan #58C4F0 | 9.9 | text |
   | Red #FF3B30 | 5.56 | text, sparingly |
   | Oxblood #A13B42 | 3.02 | large display or graphics only, never small info |

   Red on ice fails at 2.5. Dates, times and info lines go in ice or white.
7. **Dark posts in dark-mode feeds** (NN/g).
   - Every post needs one luminous element: an ice glow, a red spot or a white headline.
   - Hairlines get halation on dark, so keep hairlines for big display type only. Info lines get medium weight.
   - Keep copy short.
8. **In a dim room, reds go dark and blues stay bright (Purkinje shift).**
   - Physical red or oxblood decor and print read as near-black at night.
   - Light red things directly, or use ice or white for anything that must be seen: water, bathroom arrow, exits.
   - A red wash keeps eyes dark-adapted, so the room feels dark but people can still find their way around.
9. **Warm light on faces, cool light on walls.**
   - Ice or cyan wash goes on walls, ceiling and backdrops.
   - The DJ, bar and photo corner get warm red or amber. Blue-green on skin looks sickly.
   - This is practitioner consensus, not peer-reviewed.
10. **Colour doesn't change how hot the room is** (Sci Rep 2024). The Kruithof warm/cool "comfort curve" is debunked. The real levers are light *level* and airflow.
11. **Dark surfaces shrink a room** (design practice). Blackout fabric helps density. Keep one lit focal wall (the eye triptych) for depth.

**Myths agents must not repeat:**
- "red is sexy"
- "blue calms" as a law
- "black = luxury" as science
- the Kruithof curve
- "blue rooms feel cooler"
- 60-30-10 as science

## 7. Throwing a great party (Priya Parker, Boiler Room, club practice, safety orgs)

1. **A specific purpose that filters every decision.** "If everyone is invited, no one is invited."
   - MOTION: *"A witching-hour gallery where 70 people dance like no one's filming."* Use it to decide guests, phones, music and decor.
2. **Generous authority.** The host protects guests from boredom and bad dynamics, puts everyone on equal footing, and introduces people.
   - Put 2–3 temporary rules on the pass and at the door:
     - "Dress: all black + one red thing"
     - "No phones on the dance floor"
     - "Introduce yourself to someone new before midnight"
   - Enforce them kindly.
3. **Curate for a mix and a bit of friction.** "You always want friction… diversity" (Berghain door).
   - Invite across scenes and friend groups, not three cliques.
   - Named passes with a plus-one cap.
   - Gender and group balance is judgement, not science.
4. **Density beats size.** "A space for a gathering works best when it is contained."
   - At the Rio: furniture out, bedrooms closed off, only the living room plus the balcony open early.
   - Leave breathing room for safety.
5. **DJ in the room, not on a stage** (the Boiler Room format).
   - Booth at floor level, crowd around and behind it.
   - One fixed camera with the crowd behind the DJ (the signature shot).
   - Warm low key light on the DJ, ice wash behind.
6. **The first hour decides the night.**
   - Design the threshold: dim entry, red light, a host greeting, a stamp or token.
   - Seed 10–15 trusted early arrivals so latecomers walk into life.
   - Give early friends jobs.
   - Rohan's warm-up runs during this hour.
7. **Music: inspiration, not instructions.**
   - **DJs get inspiration, never instructions** (client rule, 5 Oct): share songs we love and the room's context, then let them play. Rohan is open format (party classics, house, Latin).
   - he/they plays when the room is fullest (~11:30–1:00).
8. **Dim, but not dark.**
   - Dim dance floor.
   - Brighter, warm bar, water, bathroom path and exits.
   - No full darkness anywhere people walk.
9. **Heat.** 70 dancers give off roughly 17 kW, about eleven space heaters.
   - Pre-cool the AC before doors.
   - Fans, plus the balcony as a cool-off zone.
   - Free water station and salty snacks.
   - DanceSafe: just under 1 L of water per hour while dancing, never more than ~1.5 L per hour.
10. **Sound and neighbours.**
    - WHO venue cap: 100 dB(A).
    - Austin quiet hours start 10:30 PM. Amplified limits are ~80 dB at the property line 10 PM–2 AM (verify current code §9-2).
    - Keep windows shut and the balcony quiet.
    - Earplugs in a bowl at the door.
    - Give neighbours a heads-up and the host's number.
11. **Phones.** Presence beats documentation (fabric, Berghain and others ban dance-floor photos).
    - MOTION runs a hybrid:
      - no phones on the floor
      - an official photographer plus the Boiler Room cam
      - a red-lit photo corner where phones are welcome
    - Ask before posting faces.
12. **Safety and consent** (Good Night Out model).
    - Two sober hosts on rotation; one is welfare + door.
    - A consent line on the pass and at the door.
    - The welfare contact's number posted. Believe people.
    - Rides confirmed and a lit pickup spot. Halloween weekend is high-risk for drunk driving.
13. **Connect people on purpose.**
    - Give roles: Water Minister, Door Witch, Intro-ducer.
    - The host makes about 10 cross-group introductions in the first two hours.
14. **Engineer the peak and the end (peak-end rule; a modest effect, but real).**
    - **Peak at midnight (the witching hour):** lights go from blue to full red, the projector eyes turn on unannounced, and the peak track plays.
    - **End at the top:** call "last song", then a slow lights-up from red to warm white. Don't fade out to five people.
15. **Close deliberately.**
    - Hosts at the door.
    - An exit token: a card with the pass number + "AFTERIMAGE 002".
    - Water and rides at the exit.
16. **Follow up.**
    - A thank-you story or DM within 48 h.
    - A recap within a week, using only the official cams and faces people agreed to.
    - Seed the next event inside the recap.
17. **Bar flow (practice only).**
    - Self-serve bar away from the DJ so queues don't block the floor.
    - Separate water.
    - Sharpies for cups.
    - Trash at each zone.
    - An ice resupply plan.

## 0. The MOTION sound (music standard v1, 5 Oct 2026; data: 598 unique songs)
1. **Club first, crowd aware:** 54% club (house, UKG, rave, classics); 41% songs everyone knows (rap, party classics, Latin).
2. **UK at heart:** garage, bass, groove house (Silva Bumpa, Josh Baker, KETTAMA, Luke Dean, Locky, Interplanetary Criminal).
3. **Known hooks, new clothes:** edits and remixes of familiar songs (mere-exposure effect + novelty).
4. **Moments, not genres:** rap, Latin and pop classics work as short shared moments.
5. **End on something everyone sings** (peak–end rule).
