# MOTION · Product Directive — Luxury Behind Affordability

The core thesis, and the cinematic direction that carries it into video. Read this before touching Higgsfield.

## The thesis

MOTION isn't selling a party. It's selling the *feeling* of a $300 door at a $0-cover price. The product is: an experience that reads as exclusive, considered, expensive — while the door stays open to anyone who gets the DM. Luxury is the texture, not the price tag. Affordability is the access, not the aesthetic.

That tension — *this looks like it costs more than it does* — is the whole brand. Every visual decision gets tested against it: does this make the night feel more valuable than what people paid to get in? If a choice reads as "cheap party" rather than "quiet luxury," it's wrong, no matter how fun it is.

## Why this is a shift, and why it's the right one

The existing Higgsfield doc (`06 MOTION — Higgsfield Video Direction`) built a *raw-cuts* reel: iPhone flash, MiniDV camcorder, grain, "not glossy CGI," "never like a nightclub ad." That's real and it stays — it's the social-native, in-the-room teaser for Stories and the hype reel after.

What you're describing now is a different object: a **hero cinematic trailer** — the one piece that establishes the brand's actual ambition before anyone sets foot in the room. Think 2009 Dior couture runway under Galliano: a single hard key light carving a figure out of total black, slow-motion fabric with nowhere to rush to, a held beat of near-silence before the reveal, an audience's anticipation shot in close-up before the garment ever appears. That's not nightlife footage — it's *theatre of the reveal*. It's the opposite instinct from raw-cuts (which hides the room until you're in it); this one stages the room like it's couture.

Both are true at once: raw-cuts says "you had to be there." The cinematic piece says "this was worth building." Run the cinematic trailer first, early in the rollout (further from the date, more polish, more patience from viewers); let raw-cuts carry the week-of urgency.

## Visual language for the cinematic cut

- **Light:** one dominant source per shot, hard-edged, the rest of the frame falls to true black. No fill. Shadows are a design element, not an accident — same instinct as the existing "deep oxblood, 10% red" rule, just lit like a runway instead of a flash photo.
- **Motion:** slow and deliberate only. Push-ins, holds, one slow orbit. No handheld, no whip, no stutter-cut — that vocabulary stays with raw-cuts. If something moves fast here, it should read as *the one moment* the restraint breaks (the drop, the reveal), not as texture.
- **Pacing:** hold shots 1–2 seconds longer than feels comfortable before the cut. Tension comes from the audience waiting, not from editing speed. A couture runway never rushes a look.
- **Palette:** unchanged — night #060B13, ice #BFDDF0 / glow #58C4F0, devil red #FF3B30 (still under ~10%, but allowed to flood once, at the climax), oxblood #A13B42. The afterimage-eyes motif (pupils dilating pinpoint → blown-out glow) is your reveal mechanism — it already *is* a couture-runway beat: anticipation, then the look.
- **Sound (for the edit):** near-silence or a single sustained low tone under the build; the only hit of music is at the reveal cut, not threaded through the whole piece.
- **What never appears:** logos/brands, the address, unconfirmed artist names, recognizable guest faces, anything that reads as a rental-hall or stock-footage nightclub. Same hard rule as raw-cuts.

## Reconciling the "no glossy CGI" rule

Raw-cuts' ban on gloss was about texture (grain, blur, phone-shot imperfection) so it reads as lived, not advertised. This piece is allowed to be polished *because it's explicitly staged as a reveal, not a documentary of the party* — the couture runway is a performance of craft, not a pretense of being candid footage. Keep it from tipping into generic luxury-brand stock look by anchoring every shot in a real MOTION asset (the eyes artwork, the actual flyer type, the actual cap) rather than inventing new imagery that isn't yours.

## Edit structure (15–20 s)

C1 hold → C2 dilation → C3 mark → C4 withheld door → C5 the one release → C6 title, held.

That's the runway structure exactly: entrance, the look, the detail, the walk, the one dramatic beat, the final stillness. Cut everything on a hard cut, no transitions — the stillness between cuts does the work a swipe or fade would otherwise do, and softening it would undercut the restraint that makes C5 land.
